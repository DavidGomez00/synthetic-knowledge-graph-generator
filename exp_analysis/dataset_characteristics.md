## Descriptors

Each dataset is a graph G = (V, R, E): V is the set of entities, R the set of relations and E the set of distinct triples (h, r, t), with h, t in V and r in R. Repeated triples are counted once. The table shows the main descriptors, and `dataset_characteristics.csv` has every one of them under the column name given in parentheses.

### Size and density

- |V| (`entities`): the number of distinct entities, those that appear as the subject or the object of a triple.
- |R| (`relations`): the number of distinct relations.
- |E| (`triples`): the number of distinct triples.
- Self-loops (`self_loops`, CSV only): the number of triples with h = t.
- Mean deg. (`mean_degree`): 2|E| / |V|, the mean number of triples an entity takes part in, as subject or object.
- Density (`density`): |E| / (|V|² |R|), the share of all possible labelled triples that are in the graph. Knowledge graphs are very sparse, so it is between 10⁻⁷ and 10⁻⁴ here.
- WCC (`largest_wcc_share`): the share of entities in the largest weakly connected component, found with union-find over the triples, ignoring their direction and relation.

### Degree heterogeneity

The in-degree of an entity is the number of triples with the entity as object, and its out-degree the number with the entity as subject, over all relations. Every entity of V is counted, including those with degree 0.

- Max in, Max out (`in_degree_max`, `out_degree_max`): the largest in- and out-degree.
- CV (`in_degree_cv`, `out_degree_cv`, CSV only): the coefficient of variation, the standard deviation of the degrees divided by their mean (|E| / |V|).
- Gini in, Gini out (`in_degree_gini`, `out_degree_gini`): the Gini coefficient of the degrees, G = 2 Σᵢ i·x₍ᵢ₎ / (n Σᵢ xᵢ) − (n + 1) / n, with the n degrees x₍₁₎ ≤ … ≤ x₍ₙ₎ sorted in ascending order. It is 0 when every entity has the same degree and approaches 1 when a few entities hold almost every triple.
- Zero share (`in_degree_zero_share`, `out_degree_zero_share`, CSV only): the share of entities that are never an object (in) or never a subject (out).

### Relation frequency distribution

The frequency f_r of a relation r is its number of triples.

- Gini rel. (`relation_freq_gini`): the Gini coefficient of the frequencies, computed as for the degrees.
- H rel. (`relation_freq_entropy`): the normalized Shannon entropy −Σ_r p_r ln p_r / ln |R|, with p_r = f_r / |E|. It is 1 when every relation has the same frequency and lower when a few relations dominate.
- Top share (`relation_freq_top_share`, CSV only): the share of triples of the most frequent relation.
- Min, median, max (`relation_freq_min`, `relation_freq_median`, `relation_freq_max`, CSV only): the smallest, median and largest frequency.

### Relation cardinality

For each relation r, tph_r = f_r / (number of distinct heads of r) is its mean number of tails per head, and hpt_r = f_r / (number of distinct tails of r) its mean number of heads per tail. Following Bordes et al. (2013), a relation is 1-1 if both are below 1.5, 1-N if only tph_r reaches 1.5, N-1 if only hpt_r does, and N-M if both do. The classes use means, so a 1-1 relation can have some heads with several tails.

- 1-1, 1-N, N-1, N-M (`relations_1-1`, …, `relations_N-M`): the number of relations of each class.
- Triple share (`triples_share_1-1`, …, `triples_share_N-M`, CSV only): the share of triples whose relation is of each class.

`relation_characteristics.csv` gives tph_r, hpt_r and the class of each relation.

### Relational patterns in the data

For each relation r, only its pairs (h, t) with h ≠ t are counted:

- symmetry: the share of these pairs whose reverse (t, h) is also in r;
- inverse: the largest share, over every other relation r', of the pairs whose reverse (t, h) is in r';
- inclusion: the largest share, over every other relation r', of the pairs (h, t) that are also in r'.

The table counts the relations whose share reaches 90%:

- Sym. (`symmetric_relations`): symmetric relations.
- Inv. (`relations_with_inverse`): relations with an inverse.
- Incl. (`contained_relations`): relations contained in another relation.
- Domain, range (`mean_domain_share`, `mean_range_share`, CSV only): the mean, over the relations, of the share of entities that are a head (domain) or a tail (range) of the relation. The datasets have no schema or type triples, so domain and range are described only by how many entities each relation uses.

`relation_characteristics.csv` gives each relation's symmetry, its closest inverse and the relation that contains the most of it, with their shares.

### Relational patterns in the mined rules

The rules are those of `<dataset>.csv`, mined by AMIE with its default settings: closed rules of at most three atoms, head coverage at least 0.01, and standard and PCA confidence at least 0.1. Each rule is classified by the shape of its atoms, with head r(x, y):

- symmetry: r(y, x) ⇒ r(x, y);
- inversion: r'(y, x) ⇒ r(x, y), with r' ≠ r;
- hierarchy: r'(x, y) ⇒ r(x, y);
- composition: two body atoms that form a path from x to y through a third variable z, in any direction, such as r₁(x, z) ∧ r₂(z, y) ⇒ r(x, y);
- intersection: two body atoms, both over x and y, such as r₁(x, y) ∧ r₂(y, x) ⇒ r(x, y);
- other: any other shape.

The columns are:

- Rules (`rules_mined`): the number of mined rules.
- Std = 1 (`rules_std1`, CSV only): the rules with standard confidence 1, the ones the generator keeps by default.
- R sym., R inv., R hier., R comp. (`rules_symmetry`, `rules_inversion`, `rules_hierarchy`, `rules_composition`): the rules of each pattern. `rules_intersection` and `rules_other` are in the CSV only.
