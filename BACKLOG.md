## 'engine/'
- [ ] **Irreflexive relations** (`core/queries.py`, `metrics.py`, `edb.py`, `generator.py`, `fill.py`): The schema (see "Schema support") states which relations are irreflexive, and that must change both how queries are built and how triples are generated. A reflexive triple (subject = object) of an irreflexive relation must never be generated, and never be taken into account as an example: not in a rule's support or groundings, and not in a predicate's metrics. The French Royalty base graph (`french_royalty.tsv`) has no reflexive triples, but a synthetic graph generated from it had 6 (2 `successor`, 2 `child`, 1 `spouse`, 1 `parent`).
  - [ ] Queries: replace the filter in `build_rule_query`, which makes every rule variable take a different value, with one `FILTER (?s != ?o)` per body or head atom whose relation is irreflexive. Variables that share no such atom may take the same value. Build this filter in one shared function and use it in every query that matches rule groundings: `build_rule_query` (completion), `get_support`, `count_producible_heads`, the groundings `generator.sample_groundings` reads, and `build_head_impact_query`/`build_body_impact_query` (`fill.py`). Then a reflexive triple of an irreflexive relation is never counted as an example of a rule, even when a real graph contains one (e.g. one loaded with `cli/complete.py`).
  - [x] EDB: each directed extensional relation gets exactly the source's number of self-loops (`edb.place_self_loops`), and random triples (`insert_random_triples`) and rule-body groundings (`generator.sample_groundings`) make no other one; direct matches already made none. Before this, 20 of the 24 benchmark copies had self-loops their source lacks, or lost the source's (`YAGO3-10` 0 → 65, `name` in `french` 122 → 0).
  - [ ] Random assignment can stop with no valid object: on 11 November 2026, the first run of `YAGO3-10_N_1` failed in `insert_random_triples` with "No set of 1 objects for `Puntland` passed the solvability check in 1000 draws" on `dealsWith` (1,258 triples, 201 subjects, 108 objects, 136 of them with `China` as object). The second run passed. The check, which since the self-loop fix counts that an entity can't be its own partner, only looks one assignment ahead, so earlier random choices can leave a state that no single choice can repair. The old code generated this dataset without the error.
  - [ ] Triple generation outside the EDB: `generator.apply_rule`, the head triples of completion, can still derive a self-loop from triples that aren't self-loops (`family`, copy of 11 November 2026: 67 `nephew`, 21 `uncle`, 11 `niece`, 4 `son`, 1 `aunt`). `fill.py`: its draws, which today follow the source graph's reflexive count per predicate.
  - [ ] Metrics: `GraphMetrics.from_uri` should leave out (and log) the reflexive triples of an irreflexive relation in the source graph, so they don't enter the target frequency, domain and range.
  - Why: with the per-atom filter in completion and in `get_support`, completion derives the head of every grounding that support can count. The exception in "Filling open relations" (`docs/concepts.md`) then disappears: random triples are safe for any relation in no kept rule's body, and the head check of `fill.py` never fires.
  - Until the schema is read, a relation with no reflexive triple in the source graph (profile `reflexivity` 0) can be treated as irreflexive.
- [ ] **Finish the fill step** (`fill.py`, phase 4 of `cli/main.py`): fill keeps closed rules' support and raises open rules' support up to their target (section 4.1 of `docs/algorithm.md`), but it stops early when a triple feeds several open rules. On `french_royalty.csv` at PCA 0.9 it leaves `father` at 271/561 and rules 10, 19 and 24 at 271/431, 54/215 and 54/200: the random pass rejects 1,915 `father` triples because of rule 10 (`father => parent`) and the rule-driven pass 970 because of rule 19 (`father & mother => spouse`).
  - [ ] Add the heads of other open rules in the same grounding: when a grounding leaves a body of another open rule without its head and that head predicate is still open, add the head too (as completion would, limited to open rules), checked with `_FillState.impact` and paid from the head predicate's budget. Today only the head of the rule being filled may be missing (`planned` in `_FillState.impact`). Expected to unblock rules 10, 19 and 24 together (`father(e,b)` with `parent(e,b)` and `spouse(a,b)`).
  - [ ] Re-run the rule-driven pass after the random pass. The random pass closes head predicates with unexplained head triples (267 `parent`, 757 `spouse` in the run above), which turns rules 10, 19 and 24 into case B1; a second pass could complete bodies for those heads. Decide whether the random pass should leave the head predicates of open rules for last, or skip them, so the B1 pass gets to choose the pairs.
  - [ ] Case A rules (every body predicate closed) are only reported. Decide whether they need anything else, e.g. a summary line, or a warning at rule-selection time.
  - [ ] Cost: every candidate triple runs one or more SPARQL queries per rule its predicate occurs in, and every rejected grounding is inserted and deleted again. Check the run time on larger rule sets and batch the checks if needed.
  - [ ] `cli/complete.py` doesn't apply the confidence filter of `--pca-conf` together with the cycle removal of a run, so it can't re-check that a synthetic graph is closed under the rules a run used. Add an option to load the same rule set as `cli/main.py` (or read `<rules>_used.csv`).
  - [ ] `get_domain`/`get_range` log a warning ("Retrieved None ...") for a predicate with no triple in the graph, which fill hits when a synthetic predicate is still empty. Return an empty dict quietly there.
- [ ] **`generator.py`** *(low priority)*: `apply_rule` inserts every new head triple, so completion can overshoot the head predicate's target frequency. A cap must check each triple against the head predicate's remaining profile (`is_assignment_solvable`) and also spend it (frequency, domain and range counts), which the unused `profile` parameter removed from `apply_rule` didn't do.
- [ ] **`metrics.py`** *(low priority)*: `GraphMetrics.from_uri` dumps its result to `logs/metrics/<uri>.json` for debugging. There's still no path to make the pipeline consume that JSON instead of re-querying the graph over SPARQL.

## Benchmark datasets
- [ ] **Name cleaning in the YAGO3-10 subsets** (`exp_datasets/`): the `1_1`, `1_N`, `N_1` and `N_M` subsets went through a cleaning step that `YAGO3-10` did not: punctuation and non-ASCII letters are gone (`101_Reykjavík` is `101_Reykjavk`, `10,000_BC_(film)` is `10000_BC_film`), so about 44,000 names per subset are not in `YAGO3-10`. The step also explains the case variants: `Kidal_(region)` and `Kidal_Region` are two names in `YAGO3-10` and became `Kidal_region` and `Kidal_Region`, so they stay separate entities (`YAGO3-10_N_M` has 60 such groups, `YAGO3-10` 14).
  - [ ] Find out whether the cleaning merged different entities. The four subsets together have 122,824 entity names and `YAGO3-10` has 123,182. Ignoring punctuation makes 362 groups of `YAGO3-10` names identical, such as `Santos_F.C.` and `Santos_FC`. Ask the team which script did the cleaning.
- [ ] **Cycle removal leaves some datasets without rules** (`core/cycles.py`): on the rules mined in `exp_datasets/`, 5 of the 24 datasets have rules with std confidence 1 and lose all of them in `remove_minimal_cyclic_rules`: `YAGO3-10` and `YAGO3-10_N_M` (3 rules each), `YAGO3-10_1_N` (1), `french1_1` (6) and `frenchN_1` (2). `SGKG1` to `SGKG4` keep 2, 1, 1 and 4 rules (from 4, 3, 3 and 6), `family` goes from 37 to 18 and `french` from 12 to 3. `WN18RR` and `WN18RRN_M` keep both their rules, which are symmetric and exempt (section 5.4 of `docs/algorithm.md`). At PCA 0.9, `WN18RR1_1`, `WN18RRN_1` and `YAGO3-10_1_1` lose all their rules. The 11 datasets with no std confidence 1 rule before the removal are an accepted outcome of the proposal; losing every rule in this step may be a limit of the approach.
  - [ ] Find which rules are removed in these datasets and why.
  - [ ] Decide whether the generator can keep more of them, for example by supporting recursive or cyclic rules during completion.
